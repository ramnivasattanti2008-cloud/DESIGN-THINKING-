Beyond Keywords: Attack Signatures for Multilingual Scam Detection

A scam detector can score 94% and still have no idea what the attack is. Here is how to measure that.

Ram Nivas Attanti
Computer Science and Business Systems
JAIN (Deemed-to-be University), Bengaluru

Abstract

Scam classifiers are evaluated on held-out samples from the same distribution they were trained on, where high scores are easy and reveal little about whether a model learned the structure of an attack or merely its vocabulary.

This work proposes the Attack Signature — a typed tuple recording who the sender impersonates, what pretext is used, which asset is sought, what the recipient is told to do, and which persuasion levers are pulled. A signature should stay the same when the message is rewritten in another language, and change when the security meaning changes.

We release Bharat-Scam-X: 1,823 labelled messages across nine Indian language varieties and twelve attack families, including 143 minimal-edit contrast pairs whose members share 55.2% of their tokens but carry opposite security meaning. Four baselines are evaluated against five diagnostic conditions.

The one-clause problem

Every pair below shares its sender, its pretext, and most of its words. One is credential theft. The other is the advisory a bank sends to prevent it. The underlined clause is the entire difference.

The operational consequence

A word-level TF-IDF classifier flags 56.0% of genuine “never share your OTP” advisories as attacks. Anti-fraud advisories are a primary defence channel. A filter with that property suppresses exactly the messages that reduce fraud.

Recombination is where it breaks

The sharpest result is not in the table above. On compositional probes — attacks built entirely from components the model saw in training, merely combined differently — exact signature recovery falls from 1.000 to 0.056.

What that means

Binary detection on those same messages stays at 0.918. The model still says “attack” while being wrong about essentially every particular of what the attack is. Real novel scams are overwhelmingly recombinations of familiar actors, pretexts and asks — so this is the failure with the most direct cost, and it is invisible to binary evaluation.

Cross-lingual consistency tracks script distance

Signature agreement is 1.000 on the three training varieties and 0.787 on the six held out. By variety: code-mixed romanised Hindi 0.750, code-mixed Telugu 0.738, Kannada 0.625, Tamil 0.613, romanised Telugu 0.475, Telugu in native script 0.450. Code-mixed text transfers best because it shares English tokens; native-script Telugu transfers worst.

The signature schema

Eight fields, each from a closed vocabulary. The six single-valued fields carry 6/8 of the agreement weight; the two set-valued fields carry 2/8.

actor  who is being impersonated pretext  the story target  asset sought intent  terminal goal action  what you must do stage  kill-chain position tactic  persuasion levers evidence  surface cues

The action vocabulary contains the value refrain — an instruction not to perform an act. That single value is what separates an attack from the advisory warning against it, and removing it collapses the framework.

What this does not show

Read this before citing anything above

Bharat-Scam-X is a constructed probe corpus, not field data. Every message is template-composed, in the tradition of SCAN, COGS and CheckList. The numbers characterise model behaviour under controlled contrast — they are not estimates of deployment performance, and a model that scores well here is not thereby shown to be deployable.

  • No neural baselines. Feature-based models only. Multilingual encoders such as MuRIL or LaBSE would likely improve the cross-lingual column substantially; establishing that is the obvious next step.
  • Template artifacts may be partly learnable. Attacks and benign items come from different generators, so a model could exploit generation quirks rather than meaning. Mitigated, not eliminated.
  • Non-English text is unreviewed. The Telugu, Tamil and Kannada realisations were authored without independent native-speaker review.
  • Small scale. Twelve families, nine varieties, 1,823 items. Bengali, Marathi, Gujarati, Malayalam, Odia and Punjabi are absent.
  • The risk function is stipulated, not calibrated against harm data — only relative comparisons are interpretable.

Reproducibility. All numbers regenerate from a single fixed seed (20260920) in about fifteen seconds on CPU: python3 src/generate.py builds the corpus, python3 src/evaluate.py prints every table.

Ethics. All messages are synthetic instances of publicly documented fraud patterns. No real institution branding, no resolving URLs, no real phone numbers, no personal data.
